# Equity Governance Implementation Standard

Status: Version 1 recommendation  
Applies to: Full Version and Essentials Edition
Last reviewed: 2026-08-18

## Purpose

This standard translates authoritative health-AI guidance into practical controls for governmental public health agencies. Equity review must operate across the AI lifecycle rather than appear as a single checklist or advisory role.

## Source foundation

The Playbook uses the following World Health Organization publications as its primary global health foundation:

- *Ethics and governance of artificial intelligence for health* (2021): human autonomy, well-being and safety, transparency, accountability, inclusiveness and equity, and sustainable and responsive AI.
- *Regulatory considerations on artificial intelligence for health* (2023): documentation and transparency, lifecycle risk management, intended use and validation, data quality, privacy and data protection, and stakeholder collaboration.
- *Ethics and governance of artificial intelligence for health: Guidance on large multi-modal models* (2024): stakeholder engagement, impact assessment, disaggregated evaluation, independent audit, and post-release monitoring for generative and multi-modal systems.
- *Health data governance in the age of artificial intelligence* (WHO Europe, 2025): representative and bias-aware data, human rights, equity, and accountable health-data governance.
- *Equity across the regulation, implementation and evaluation of digital health* (WHO Europe, 2026): equity-by-design, inclusive governance, participatory design, equity metrics, and bias auditing across the technology lifecycle.

The workshop's “Six Domains × Ten Practices” framework and “E-FAIR” model regulation are treated as the workshop authors' implementation products unless their publishers document otherwise. They must not be described as WHO frameworks or reproduced without authorization.

## Required lifecycle controls

### Problem definition and data

- Identify who may receive benefits and who may bear burdens or errors.
- Identify subgroups and relevant intersections rather than relying only on one demographic characteristic at a time.
- Review proxies such as cost, utilization, attendance, digital engagement, or prior service use for structural access barriers.
- Apply Tribal, community, and jurisdiction-specific data-governance requirements.

### Design, procurement, and validation

- Define intended use, prohibited use, human decision authority, and local validation population.
- Require disaggregated and intersectional performance evidence with sample-size limitations.
- Require vendor bias-audit evidence, model and data documentation, limitations, and model-change notification.
- Establish measurable equity thresholds and no-go, pause, or remediation criteria before deployment.
- Give affected communities a documented participation role appropriate to the level of impact.

### Deployment, monitoring, and correction

- Monitor benefit, burden, error, access, and outcome measures by relevant subgroup and intersection.
- Set an audit cadence, independent review responsibility, escalation path, and public or community reporting plan.
- Record corrective actions, owners, deadlines, and evidence of closure.
- Define consequences when thresholds or contractual requirements are not met, including conditions, suspension, replacement, or retirement.

## Tool implementation

The controls are implemented through a connected trio of existing tools:

1. Tool 3, Equity Impact Assessment Checklist, documents the pre-approval context, structural and proxy risks, intersectional evidence, community authority, thresholds, and conditions.
2. Tool 32, Vendor Evaluation Checklist, records vendor evidence, independent audits, subgroup performance, contract rights, remediation deadlines, and consequences.
3. Tool 47, Equity & Disparity Impact Monitoring, tracks post-deployment equity measures, triggers, community feedback, corrective action, escalation, and retirement decisions.

Essentials Edition tools for Plays 4, 7, and 13 ask the corresponding participation, screening, and monitoring questions without exposing the Full Version tool set.

## Evidence and publication rules

- Cite the original source for quantitative claims and verify that the population, setting, denominator, and publication date match the claim.
- Do not publish the workshop's “11 of 194,” “70 percent,” or “3 percent” figures as established facts until their underlying studies or datasets are identified and reviewed.
- Clearly distinguish binding law, formal regulation, voluntary guidance, scoping review findings, workshop tools, and Playbook recommendations.
- Review legal applicability locally; WHO publications and this standard do not create binding authority.

## Remaining recommendations

- Pilot the three-tool lifecycle with one realistic public health scenario and verify that findings carry forward without re-entry.
- Add machine-readable links between Tool 3 conditions, Tool 32 contract requirements, and Tool 47 monitoring thresholds when the shared content model is implemented.
- Recruit reviewers representing community engagement, civil rights, disability access, Tribal data sovereignty, epidemiology, program operations, procurement, legal/privacy, and model evaluation.
- Require a named role and decision authority instead of assigning responsibility to a generic “equity” or “governance” group.
- Reassess the standard annually and whenever material WHO guidance, applicable law, or field evidence changes.
