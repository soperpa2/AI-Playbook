# Curriculum Source Alignment: 2026 Update

Status: Review draft  
Last updated: 2026-08-20

## Purpose

This update aligns the AI Playbook curriculum with the CDC AI Strategy for FY 2026–2030, current WHO health-AI ethics and equity guidance, and HHS Office for Civil Rights guidance relevant to public health, HIPAA, business associates, and cloud services.

## Updated modules

### Foundational and intake

- INT 100: CDC four-pillar strategy and public health context.
- INT 105: safe AI use beyond HIPAA, approved environments, and vendor retention or model-training risks.
- INT 200: authority, permitted purpose, vendor processing, agentic autonomy, sustainability, measurable value, and Tool 10 routing.
- INT 250: required all-staff learning on AI lifecycle impacts, energy, water, emissions, hardware, e-waste, environmental justice, proportionality, lower-impact alternatives, vendor evidence, uncertainty, and monitoring. Full title: *Environmental and Resource Impacts of AI - Considerations for Ethical Public Health Use*.
- GOV 100: risk-proportionate governance, public trust, third-party review, and named decision authority.

### Governance, privacy, equity, and procurement

- GOV 310: covered, non-covered, and hybrid entities; public health authority; permitted purpose; minimum necessary and data minimization; business associates; BAAs; vendor processing; and Tool 10.
- GOV 330: FAIR principles, enterprise platforms, provenance, purpose limitations, equity, and community or Tribal governance.
- PGM 210: testable privacy, security, equity, evidence, audit, remediation, suspension, termination, and exit requirements.
- ANL 330: intersectionality, structural inequity, proxy variables, benefit and burden measures, community-defined concerns, thresholds, and monitoring.
- POL 330: lifecycle equity policy, civil rights, accessibility, authority, vendor evidence, enforceability, and accurate source labeling.
- ANL 300: local, disaggregated, intersectional, generative, and multi-modal validation.

### Implementation, architecture, operations, and workforce

- ARC 220: interoperable AI-ready data with authority, provenance, access, logging, and downstream-use controls.
- ARC 320: bounded agentic tools, permissions, approval gates, observable actions, rollback, and incident response.
- OPS 300: reproducible dataset, model, prompt, retrieval, platform, and approval lineage.
- OPS 310: public health value, burden reduction, timeliness, prevention, detection, response, equity, cost, and sustainability measures.
- EXE 440: foundational and role-based learning, advanced expertise, communities of practice, bridge roles, recruitment, retention, assignments, and fellowships.

## Template review decision

The course-page template is in review status. The streamlined design uses:

1. A compact course header and download area.
2. A five-step navigation strip: Objectives, Learn, Apply, Check, Resources.
3. Three at-a-glance facts: estimated time, level, and audience.
4. Plain-language statements of when to use the course and what the learner will complete.
5. Collapsed prerequisites unless preparation is required.
6. One open instructional section at a time.
7. Practical application and knowledge check after the instructional content.
8. References, glossary, policy notes, and crosswalks after the core learning flow.

PowerPoint and PDF course publications will be regenerated after the template is approved so all formats use the accepted information hierarchy.

## Source interpretation

- CDC federal operating requirements are not presented as automatically binding on STLT agencies.
- WHO publications are identified as guidance rather than binding law.
- HHS/OCR materials are used to explain HIPAA applicability and permissions; learners must confirm agency- and jurisdiction-specific requirements with authorized officials.
- External workshop frameworks are not attributed to WHO unless their provenance is documented.
- Environmental claims distinguish measured evidence from estimates and disclose relevant boundaries and uncertainty. INT 250 uses APHA public-health materials, WHO health-AI ethics and sustainability guidance, UNEP lifecycle analysis, UNESCO's AI and environment framework, GAO's technology assessment, and IEA energy-system evidence.
